# Cell Theory

## One-sentence definition
> All living organisms are composed of one or more cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

## Full explanation
The modern cell theory has three core tenets:
1. All living things are made of cells.
2. The cell is the fundamental unit of structure and function in living organisms.
3. All cells come from other cells by division (omnis cellula e cellula).

Later additions include the ideas that cells contain hereditary information and that energy flow occurs within cells. The theory unified the plant and animal kingdoms and provided the foundation for all modern biology.

## Feynman-style explanation
Your body is a gigantic city built by trillions of tiny living workers called cells. Each worker can eat, breathe, make decisions at a molecular level, and divide into two copies of itself. Whether you look at an elephant or a bacterium, everything alive is made of these same basic living units. No cell ever appears out of thin air — every one comes from a previous cell.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Bricks of a building** — A castle and a hut are both made of the same kind of bricks (cells).
2. **City of workers** — Trillions of specialized workers cooperating, each able to reproduce.
3. **Matryoshka dolls of life** — Life is nested; the smallest independent living unit is the cell.

## Concrete examples

1. **Human body** — Approximately 30–40 trillion human cells plus a similar number of bacterial cells.
2. **Bacterial culture** — A single bacterium can give rise to billions through repeated division.
3. **Wound healing** — Damaged tissue is repaired by existing cells dividing and migrating.

## Quantitative / data anchors
- Typical animal cell diameter: 10–30 μm
- Number of cells in an adult human: ~3.7 × 10¹³ (human) + comparable microbial cells

## Historical & discovery context
Schleiden (plants, 1838) and Schwann (animals, 1839) proposed that cells are the basic units. Virchow (1855) added that cells only come from other cells, rejecting spontaneous generation for cells.

## Relationships

### Builds on / presupposes
- Microscopy and the observation of cells

### Influences / leads to
- All of cellular and molecular biology
- Germ theory of disease
- Understanding of cancer (uncontrolled cell division)

### Contrasts with
- Pre-cell theories of vitalism and spontaneous generation

## Common misconceptions
- “Viruses are cells.” → Viruses are not cellular and are generally not considered living under the cell theory.
- “All cells look the same.” → Enormous diversity of form and function.
