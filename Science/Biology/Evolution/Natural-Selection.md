# Principle of Evolution by Natural Selection

## One-sentence definition
> Organisms with heritable traits that confer a survival or reproductive advantage in a given environment tend to leave more offspring, causing those traits to become more common over generations.

## Full explanation
Natural selection is the differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype. When variation is heritable, the frequency of advantageous alleles increases in the population. It is not random: the variation is random (or undirected), but the filtering by the environment is directional. Over long timescales this process produces adaptation and, given isolation and time, speciation.

Key requirements: variation, heritability, and differential reproductive success.

## Feynman-style explanation
Imagine a population of bears in a snowy place. Some are born a bit whiter, some a bit darker. The white ones are harder for prey to see, so they eat better and have more cubs. The dark ones go hungry more often. Over many generations, almost all the bears become white because the “white” recipes were passed on more successfully. The environment acted like a filter.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Coffee filter** — The environment lets only certain traits “pass through” to the next generation.
2. **Sieve** — Variation is poured in; only the variants that fit the holes (environmental demands) continue.
3. **Blind watchmaker** (Dawkins) — Complex design arises without a designer through cumulative selection.

## Concrete examples

1. **Peppered moths** — During the Industrial Revolution, dark moths became more common in polluted areas because they were better camouflaged against soot-darkened trees.
2. **Antibiotic resistance** — Bacteria with chance mutations that resist an antibiotic survive and multiply when the antibiotic is present.
3. **Darwin’s finches** — Beak shapes changed measurably within years in response to drought and seed availability (Grant studies).

## Quantitative / data anchors
- Change in allele frequency can be modeled by the Price equation or simpler population-genetics models.
- Selection coefficient \( s \) quantifies the relative fitness advantage.

## Historical & discovery context
Darwin and Wallace independently arrived at the idea. Darwin published *On the Origin of Species* in 1859. The modern synthesis later integrated it with Mendelian genetics.

## Common misconceptions
- “Evolution is just a theory” (in the scientific sense, theory means a well-supported explanatory framework).
- “Natural selection is random.” → Mutation is undirected; selection is not.
- “It produces perfection.” → It produces local, contingent adaptations, often with trade-offs.
