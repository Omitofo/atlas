# Conservation of Energy

## One-sentence definition
> The total energy of an isolated system remains constant; energy can change form but cannot be created or destroyed.

## Full explanation
The First Law of Thermodynamics states that the change in internal energy of a system equals the heat added to the system minus the work done by the system (ΔU = Q − W). More broadly, across all of physics, the total energy — kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electromagnetic, mass-energy (E=mc²), etc. — is conserved in a closed system. This is a direct consequence of time-translation symmetry via Noether’s theorem.

## Feynman-style explanation
Energy is like a set of identical LEGO bricks that the universe is made of. You always have exactly the same number of bricks. You can build a car, smash it, and rebuild a house, but you never lose or gain a single brick. The form changes; the total amount never does.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **LEGO bricks of the universe** — Always the same number, different constructions.
2. **Bank account** — You can move money between checking, savings, and investments, but the total balance stays the same unless you add or remove from outside.
3. **Water in connected vessels** — Water can flow from one container to another, but the total volume remains constant if the system is sealed.

## Concrete examples

1. **Roller coaster** — Gravitational potential energy at the top converts to kinetic energy at the bottom, then partially to heat via friction in the brakes.
2. **Photosynthesis & respiration** — Light energy is stored as chemical energy in glucose; later released as usable energy (ATP) and heat.
3. **Nuclear reactions** — A tiny amount of mass is converted into a large amount of energy (E=mc²), but total mass-energy is conserved.

## Quantitative / data anchors
- First Law: ΔU = Q − W
- Mechanical energy (ideal): E = K + U = constant
- Mass-energy equivalence: E = mc²

## Historical & discovery context
Independent work by Mayer (1842), Joule (precise measurements of the mechanical equivalent of heat), and Helmholtz (1847 formal statement). The principle unified mechanics, heat, electricity, and chemistry.

## Common misconceptions
- “Energy can be used up.” → It is transformed, often into less useful forms (see Second Law).
- “Perpetual motion machines of the first kind are possible.” → They would violate conservation of energy.
