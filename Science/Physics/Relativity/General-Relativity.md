# Principle of General Relativity

## One-sentence definition
> Gravity is not a force acting at a distance, but the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.

## Full explanation
In General Relativity, the presence of mass-energy tells spacetime how to curve, and the curvature of spacetime tells matter how to move. The Einstein field equations relate the geometry of spacetime (described by the Einstein tensor) to the distribution of energy and momentum (the stress-energy tensor). Free-falling objects follow geodesics — the straightest possible paths in curved spacetime.

This replaces Newton’s inverse-square force law with a geometric description. In the weak-field, low-velocity limit it recovers Newtonian gravity, but it also predicts new phenomena: light deflection, gravitational time dilation, black holes, gravitational waves, and the expansion of the universe.

## Feynman-style explanation
Imagine spacetime as a big flexible trampoline or a sheet of rubber. When you put something heavy (the Sun) on it, the sheet bends. A smaller marble (the Earth) doesn’t get “pulled” by a mysterious force — it simply rolls downhill along the curved surface. That’s gravity. The heavier the object, the deeper the dent, and the more everything nearby has to follow the slope.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Trampoline / rubber sheet** — Heavy objects create dents; lighter objects follow the curves.
2. **River current** — Space itself is flowing toward massive objects; you are carried along even if you try to stand still.
3. **Gelatin universe** — Massive bodies sink into the cosmic gelatin and drag the surrounding medium with them.

## Concrete examples

1. **Mercury’s perihelion precession** — Newtonian gravity could not fully explain the slow rotation of Mercury’s orbit. General Relativity accounts for the extra 43 arcseconds per century.
2. **Gravitational lensing** — Light from distant galaxies is bent around massive clusters, creating multiple images or arcs (observed by Hubble and JWST).
3. **GPS satellites** — Clocks on GPS satellites run faster than clocks on Earth due to both special-relativistic velocity effects and general-relativistic gravitational potential. Without relativistic corrections, GPS would drift by kilometers per day.

## Quantitative / data anchors
- Einstein field equations: \( G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu} \)
- Schwarzschild radius: \( r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} \)
- Light deflection by the Sun: 1.75 arcseconds (confirmed 1919)

## Historical & discovery context
Einstein published the final form of the field equations in November 1915. The 1919 solar eclipse expedition led by Arthur Eddington provided the first major observational confirmation (light bending). Later confirmations include the Pound-Rebka experiment (gravitational redshift), Hulse-Taylor pulsar (gravitational waves via orbital decay), and the direct detection of gravitational waves by LIGO in 2015.

## Relationships

### Builds on / presupposes
- Special Relativity (constancy of the speed of light, spacetime)
- Equivalence Principle (local equivalence of gravity and acceleration)

### Influences / leads to
- Black hole physics
- Cosmology (expanding universe, Big Bang models)
- Modern GPS and precision timing systems

### Contrasts with
- Newtonian gravity (action at a distance, absolute space and time)

## Common misconceptions
- “Gravity is still a force, just a more complicated one.” → In GR it is geometry.
- “Spacetime is a physical substance you can touch.” → It is a mathematical model of relationships between events.
- “Black holes suck everything in like vacuum cleaners.” → They curve spacetime extremely strongly; crossing the event horizon is not special locally.
