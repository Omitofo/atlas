# Law of Conservation of Mass

## One-sentence definition
> In a closed chemical system, the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products; mass is neither created nor destroyed in chemical reactions.

## Full explanation
During any chemical reaction in a sealed container, atoms are rearranged but not created or destroyed. Therefore the total mass remains constant. This principle was crucial in establishing chemistry as a quantitative science and in overthrowing the phlogiston theory. In modern terms it is an approximation that holds extremely well for chemical (non-nuclear) processes; at the nuclear level, mass and energy are interconvertible.

## Feynman-style explanation
Matter is like a set of Lego bricks of different colors. When you take apart a castle and build boats instead, you still have exactly the same number and types of bricks. Nothing disappears and nothing new appears out of nowhere. The total weight stays the same.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Lego castle → boats** — Same bricks, different arrangement, same total mass.
2. **Balanced chemical equation as a recipe** — Ingredients on the left must equal products on the right in atoms (and therefore mass).
3. **Closed box** — Whatever happens inside a sealed box, the weight of the box does not change.

## Concrete examples

1. **Burning wood in a sealed container** — The mass of ash + smoke + gases equals the original mass of wood + oxygen.
2. **Rusting of iron** — Iron + oxygen → iron oxide; the oxide weighs more than the original iron by exactly the mass of oxygen that combined.
3. **Precipitation reactions** — Mixing two clear solutions produces a solid, but the total mass of the mixture remains constant.

## Quantitative / data anchors
- In a balanced equation the sum of atomic masses on both sides is identical (within measurement error).
- Modern mass spectrometry confirms conservation to extremely high precision for chemical reactions.

## Historical & discovery context
Antoine Lavoisier carefully weighed reactants and products in closed vessels and showed that mass is conserved. This work (published in 1789 in *Traité Élémentaire de Chimie*) is considered the birth of modern chemistry.

## Relationships

### Builds on / presupposes
- Atomic theory (atoms are conserved)

### Influences / leads to
- Stoichiometry and quantitative chemistry
- Later refined by mass-energy equivalence in relativity

### Contrasts with
- Nuclear reactions (where measurable mass is converted to energy)

## Common misconceptions
- “Mass is lost when something burns.” → The gases produced account for the “missing” mass.
- “Conservation of mass is the same as conservation of energy.” → Related but distinct; the deeper principle is conservation of mass-energy.
