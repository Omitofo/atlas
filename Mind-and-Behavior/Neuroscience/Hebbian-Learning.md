# Hebbian Learning (Neuronal Plasticity)

## One-sentence definition
> Synaptic connections between neurons strengthen when the neurons are activated repeatedly at the same time — “neurons that fire together, wire together.”

## Full explanation
Donald Hebb proposed that the persistence of activity in a neural circuit leads to lasting cellular changes that increase the efficiency of that circuit. In modern terms, when a presynaptic neuron repeatedly takes part in firing a postsynaptic neuron, the synapse between them is strengthened (long-term potentiation, LTP). The converse (neurons that fire out of sync lose their link) also occurs. This local, unsupervised learning rule is a fundamental mechanism of learning, memory, and the formation of neural assemblies.

## Feynman-style explanation
Your brain is full of tiny wires that talk to each other. When you practice something — a song, a tennis stroke, a new language — the same sets of wires keep lighting up together. Each time they do, the connection between them gets a little stronger and faster, like a path through tall grass that becomes a smooth trail the more you walk it. Eventually the skill feels automatic.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Path through tall grass** — Repeated use turns a faint trail into a clear road.
2. **Muscle that grows with use** — Synapses, like muscles, hypertrophy with repeated activation.
3. **Friendship that deepens with shared experience** — Neurons that “spend time together” form stronger bonds.

## Concrete examples

1. **Learning to ride a bicycle** — Initially clumsy circuits become highly efficient through practice; the skill persists for decades.
2. **Language acquisition** — Phoneme maps in the auditory cortex are shaped by the statistical patterns of the language heard in early life.
3. **London taxi drivers** — Posterior hippocampus enlarges with the years of spatial navigation training (Maguire studies).

## Quantitative / data anchors
- Long-term potentiation (LTP) can increase synaptic strength by 50–200% or more.
- Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) has precise millisecond windows.

## Historical & discovery context
Hebb published *The Organization of Behavior* in 1949. The cellular mechanism (LTP) was discovered in the hippocampus by Bliss and Lømo in 1973, confirming Hebb’s intuition.

## Relationships

### Builds on / presupposes

### Influences / leads to

- Understanding of critical periods and developmental plasticity

### Contrasts with
- Purely feed-forward, non-plastic models of the brain

## Common misconceptions
- “Hebbian learning is the only form of plasticity.” → There are many forms (homeostatic, neuromodulatory, structural, etc.).
- “It only strengthens connections.” → Depression of unused synapses is equally important.
